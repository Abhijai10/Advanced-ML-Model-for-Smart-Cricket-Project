# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Smart Cricket is for cricket players, coaches, and reviewers who want a simple way to record a batting shot, run an AI-assisted analysis, and review the result without reading ML internals.

Users are likely working in a practice setting: nets, a training lane, a coaching session, or a laptop review after recording. They need clear answers quickly: what shot was played, how confident the model was, what the technique score was, and what coaching feedback should be practiced next.

## Product Purpose

The product turns cricket shot video into an analysis experience: capture or upload a batting motion, send it through the Smart Cricket backend, show the predicted shot and coaching feedback, and save analysis history for progress tracking.

Success means the interface feels like a credible project website and app shell: understandable to non-ML viewers, useful for demos, and honest about what the current model can analyze.

## Brand Personality

Confident, athletic, precise.

The product should feel like a polished sports-tech tool rather than a research notebook. It should be visually sharp enough for a portfolio/demo while staying practical for actual repeated use.

## Anti-references

Avoid student-project dashboards, generic AI landing pages, fake SaaS pricing pages, overcomplicated ML terminology, neon gamer styling, cricket cliches as decoration, and placeholder login screens.

Avoid claiming professional coaching certainty. The app can present model output and technique feedback, but it should not imply certified medical or elite coaching diagnosis.

## Design Principles

Show the product working before explaining the research.

Keep the cricket story understandable: capture, analyze, review, improve.

Make the authenticated app feel operational, with real controls, history, and feedback states.

Protect trust by being precise about model outputs and by avoiding overclaims.

Use the creator credit clearly: made by Abhijai Raghuvanshi.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-accessible controls. Camera, upload, and analysis actions must expose clear loading, error, and empty states. Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences. Feedback should be visible as text even when voice/audio output is unavailable.
